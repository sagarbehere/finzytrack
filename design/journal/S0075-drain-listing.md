# S0075: the drain's member listing restored, with the zombie exclusion

## Brief
- Base: e92a4e1d.
- Goal: bring back the process-group drain of dfe3aee8 (hold the exited leader unreaped; signal the group and list its members, macOS libproc, Linux `/proc`, until no member but the held leader is left; then reap; never signal after the reap), so that a plugin child forked while the group's SIGKILL is delivered (macOS) cannot survive the drain (BL-0190). With it BL-0158: a zombie member whose parent left the group and never reaps it must not keep the macOS drain going; exclude listed members whose status reads zombie, still counting an exiting member that is not yet one. ## Decisions
- **BL-0158's status read comes from `sysctl(KERN_PROC_PID)`, not `proc_pidinfo`** (agent, after a probe; the remedy's rule holds, its call does not). On this Mac (macOS 26.6) `proc_pidinfo(pid, PROC_PIDTBSDINFO)` and `PROC_PIDT_SHORTBSDINFO` fail with `ESRCH` for a zombie: the row's remedy, taken literally, would never see one. `ESRCH` cannot be read as "zombie" either, since a process in the middle of exiting may also be unfindable there, and an exiting member must count (its files may still be open; S0057's 36 of 40). `sysctl(CTL_KERN, KERN_PROC, KERN_PROC_PID, pid)` returns the `kinfo_proc` of a zombie too (what `ps` reads), with `kp_proc.p_stat == SZOMB` (5) only once the process has exited (the kernel closes its files before it sets `SZOMB`). Only a positive read excludes a member: a status that cannot be read, a size too small, or a pid in the record other than the one asked for (a layout check of offsets 36 and 40 of the 64-bit `kinfo_proc`) counts as live. Probe: live child `p_stat` 2, zombie 5, reaped size 0.
- **Departures from dfe3aee8's drain** (agent; for the orchestrator's red-team call): (1) `live_member_in_group_listing` takes a third argument, the listed pids whose status reads zombie, and does not count them; (2) `PosixGroupOps.live_member` reads each listed member's status but the leader's with the new `status_reads_zombie` (macOS only); (3) docstrings. The loop, the pauses, the leader's held zombie, the leader check, the Linux path, `signal()`, the reap and "never signal after the reap" are dfe3aee8's code, byte for byte. Kept from S0073: the Cancel rule (queue table version 9), its texts and tests, the spec's rules-only form; S0073's test for a frozen leader keeps both checks it had (`leader_exited` false while stopped, and the restored `live_member` true).
- **`end_own_group` stays one signal; a §15 limit and BL-0194** (agent). The engine's own end (parent-death watchdog; end of input) is `killpg(0, SIGKILL)`, the engine included. At end of input the sidecar is alive and its reaper drains the group with the listing, so only the watchdog case (the sidecar is gone) has no drain after the signal. A fix there needs a process that outlives the engine to keep signalling (a helper that leaves the group and signals until `ESRCH`, whose `EPERM` answers on macOS are ambiguous, as above), or the next start to signal a group it did not start: each is a new mechanism in a dying process, not cheap, and the second breaks "never signal a process we did not start". The case needs a sidecar death and a plugin child's fork in the same instant; the block it causes fails closed (the next start waits at the engine lock with the advice). Spec §15 says so, and ARCHITECTURE.md and the engine's docstring point to it. **Corrected in round 1 (verifier F3):** "only the watchdog case" was wrong. At a normal quit and at Quit anyway `close()` sends one group signal and the sidecar exits without waiting for the drain (it runs on the daemon reaper thread), and the SIGKILLed engine runs no `end_own_group`, so the quit path has the same one-signal exposure; spec §5.2, §9.3 step 4, §15, failure-modes E7, ARCHITECTURE.md and BL-0194 now name it. Quit behaviour is unchanged: making quit wait for the drain touches ADR-0063 decision 1 step 4 and is the owner's call (offered by the orchestrator).
- **§5.1's "Its process group holds it" and §5.2's "cannot outlive their engine"** (agent): §5.1 now says a plain-`fork` child shares the lock and the drain ends every such member before the reap; the sidecar's wait at the lock covers what the drain cannot reach (a child that left the group, one that survived the engine's own end). §5.2 says the end signals until no live member is left, "none outlives its engine's drain", and names the `setsid` exception (§15).
- **The fork-race tests are made reliable by repetition, not made deterministic** (agent): the race is in the kernel's delivery of one `killpg` during a `fork`; nothing in user space can place a fork inside it. Measured on e92a4e1d with the tests' own scripts (20 runs each): members alive at once after the drain 17/20 (lock) and 17/20 (no lock); alive 1 s later 10/20 and 14/20; lock held 1 s later 10/20. The tests run 10 times each and fail at the first survivor, so a one-signal drain passes one with a chance of about 0.15^10. Each forked worker sleeps 30 s at most, and every run ends its own group (`killpg` until `ESRCH`/`EPERM`, only on the group of a leader the test started and reaped while members keep the id) and checks with `ps` that the group is empty.
- **The BL-0158 real-process test is a `@regression-guard`** (agent): it passes on the base (one signal, no listing) and fails on dfe3aee8's drain (the drain waited the 20 s deadline, then the test failed); its pure counterpart fails on the base at import.
- **Retired terms un-retired** (agent): "no live member", `live_member`, `live_member_in_group_listing`, `live_member_in_proc` and the libproc listing names, which S0073 retired, are removed from `ACTIVE`; their meta-test samples go and two trap lines with the restored words are added (they would be refused on the base).
- **BL-0185 grep** (agent), over `engine/src`, `backend/src`, `scripts`, `formal/tla/engine-jobs/*.md`, for `signals? the group once|still exiting|one signal|no listing|member.{0,40}engine lock|drained|lock wait|before every fresh engine`: the "signals ... once" hits are our end's single signal (`end`, `close()`, the table's `End`: true, the drain does the rest); `engine_client`'s module docstring and `protocol.server.end_own_group` were changed; the rest hold.
- **failure-modes.md row E2** (agent): its sentence and test reference were S0073's ("no longer holds the drain", the one-signal test); back to "keeps the drain going" and `test_a_member_that_never_ends_keeps_the_drain_going_and_the_leader_unreaped` (`check_failure_modes` refused the gone test name).
- **The full backend suite's one failure is BL-0176** (agent): `tests/test_main.py::test_a_signal_at_any_line_of_the_start_never_skips_the_engines_close` failed once in the full run and in 3 of 12 isolated reruns right after it (no log lines at all: the start-mode signal storm), then 0 of 50 isolated reruns, and failed again in a second full run (`-n 8`). The base source (e92a4e1d) under the same parallel run fails it too, 2 of 2 (isolated: 0 of 12). Its failure shows no log file, so no engine was involved; it is the known start-storm flake of BL-0176, worse under load.
