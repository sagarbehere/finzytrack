# S0057: The engine client without time limits, in its own process group, with the commit-point protocol

## Brief
- Goal: the first of BL-0150's two slices: the engine-jobs spec's §5.5 changes to the built engine client and engine (`design/specs/engine-jobs.md` §5.1-§5.5, §11, §14 "Engine"; ADR-0064), so the scheduler shell of the second slice (S0058) has a client it can drive:
  - **No time limits** (§5.4, §5.5 bullet 1): `EngineClient.call` and `ping` take no timeout; `TimedOut`, the handshake limit `start_timeout`, `close_timeout` and the `RestartPolicy.longest_*` bounds leave the statechart and the client. A request, a start and a handshake wait until the engine answers, ends, or is ended by us.
  - **Our ends, recorded before the signal** (§5.2, §5.3): `EngineClient.end(reason)` (`cancel`, `retire`, `quit_anyway`) records the reason and signals the engine's group once, and never waits; callable from another thread while a call is in flight (a start, a handshake, a backoff pause or a request), so a waiting job's Cancel ends even a start that never completes. In the statechart, `Cancelled` (reason `cancel` or `quit_anyway`) and `Retired` join as events; neither is a stop; the caller of an ended call gets `EngineFailure("cancelled")`. `Close` is also accepted in the passing states, where the call in flight observes the end that `close()` signalled.
  - **The engine leads its own process group** (§5.2): spawned with `start_new_session`; every end of ours signals the whole group; after the leader exits it is held unreaped (`waitid(..., WEXITED | WNOWAIT)`), the group is signalled until it has **no live member** (macOS: `killpg(pgid, 0)` answers `ESRCH` or `EPERM`; Linux: no non-zombie process in `/proc` has that group), then `wait4` reaps it; after the reap no signal is ever sent to that group. This runs on a reaper thread per engine, behind a small port of group operations so a fake can record every signal. A crashed engine is drained the same way. The client reports each engine's end (`on_engine_ended`, with its spawn serial, reason, exit status and peak memory) once its group is drained and reaped: the hook S0058 turns into the queue's `EngineEnded` and `EngineDied`. A new engine is spawned only after the previous one's drain has finished (§5.1, `AtMostOneEngine`).
  - **The engine ends its own group** (§5.2): its parent-death watchdog and its end-of-input exit call `killpg(0, SIGKILL)` when the engine leads its group (otherwise, run by hand or in a test, it only exits, so it can never kill a group it does not lead).
  - **`close()` without waiting** (§5.5 bullet 2, §9.3 step 4): it records `close`, signals the group once and returns: it no longer takes the call lock (a call in flight to a hung engine cannot hold it) and never waits for the reap.
  - **The spawn** (§5.1, §5.5 bullet 4): `-s -S -B -P` with the bootstrap that adds only the engine's site-packages, in development too (the venv's site-packages), never `-I`; the environment is the parent's minus `FT_*` and every `PYTHON*` variable, plus `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`. **The bytecode directory** (`app_local_data/engine/bytecode/<build>/`): filled once per build in the background by `compileall` under `-X pycache_prefix` (the engine's interpreter library, site-packages and code; never a user's folder), marked complete last, then used read-only with `-X pycache_prefix`; an engine that fails to start while using it gets it deleted and is started once without it, not counted as a stop.
  - **The protocol, version 2** (§5.4, §5.5 bullet 5, §6.1 steps 7 and 9, §12): progress messages (`event: progress` with a phase), `event: gate` (the engine waits) and the sidecar's `control: proceed`, `event: committed`, and an optional `decision {table, version, inputs, output}` on replies. In the engine a handler gets a job context (`progress`, `gate`, `committed`, `decide`); at the gate the next line must be `proceed` for that request: end of input does the end-of-input exit (never proceeds), any other line refuses the request (never proceeds). In the client, `call(..., on_event=…)` delivers progress, gate, committed and decision; `proceed()` sends `proceed` only while a gate is pending, else it does nothing and says so; a second gate, or `committed` before `proceed`, is a protocol violation. **The violation rule after the commit point** (§5.3 table): a reply that breaks the protocol after `proceed` does not signal the engine: its input is closed, its output read and discarded until it exits, and the stop is counted at the exit.
- Split (owner's instruction, 2026-10-29, for BL-0150): this slice is the client and engine half; S0058, later, is the scheduler shell in the user container, the queue codes, the engine-lock wait before every spawn, death reports by engine number, recover-all's Cancel between engines, the spawn-on-demand that replaces `EngineBoot`'s start at boot, and the model-driven conformance tests. **Moved across the line (agent):** failure-modes rows E1, E2, E3, E5, E6 and E7 change in this slice, not in S0058, as far as the client and engine behaviour goes: they describe the built client (the time limit, `TimedOut`, the bounded close) and cite tests this slice removes, and the failure-modes check fails on a row that names a transition the statechart no longer has. What the user sees (messages, codes) stays planned (BL-0092, S0058).
- Also (owner decision, 2026-10-29: "Drop it, keep R4 (Recommended)"): the spec's Terms list (§1) gains "**R4:** the re-check after a save (§6.7); the label is kept for historical reasons." R4 is renamed nowhere.
- Implements: ADR-0064 (decisions 1, 3, 4 as they concern the engine process) and the spec's §5.1 (spawn flags, environment, bytecode directory), §5.2 (reasons, group, drain before reap, no live member per OS, the engine's own group end), §5.3 (outcome table rows: `cancelled`, our `retire`/`close`, the violation after the commit point), §5.4 (no time limits; progress), §5.5 bullets 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; ADR-0054 decision 5 (the engine's own closure only, now under `-S` in development too).

## Decisions
- **R4 keeps its name** (owner, 2026-10-29: "Drop it, keep R4 (Recommended)", answering whether to finish renaming R4 to "re-check"; the option read: keep R4 and add a glossary line). The spec's Terms list (§1) gains "**R4:** the re-check after a save (§6.7); the label is kept for historical reasons." R4 is renamed nowhere.
- **The split line moved for the failure-mode rows** (agent): the orchestrator put failure-modes E2, E3, E5, E7 in S0058, but those rows describe the built client (the time limit, `TimedOut`, the bounded close) and cite tests this slice removes, and `scripts/check_failure_modes.py` fails on a transition the statechart no longer has. So E1, E2, E3, E5, E6 and E7 change here for the client and the engine; what the user sees (messages, codes) stays with S0058 and BL-0092. Nothing else moved: the scheduler shell, the codes, spawn on demand and the conformance tests stay in S0058.
- **Our ends are events of the call they end** (agent; spec §5.2, §5.3, §5.5): `end(reason)` and `close()` never take the call lock. They record the reason on the running engine (only if its leader has not already exited: an engine that died by itself keeps that truth and stays a stop) and signal its group; a call in flight observes the end of the output and the table calls it `Cancelled` (or `Close`); a call with no engine yet (a backoff, a start, waiting for the previous drain) sees `_pending` and ends there. With no call in flight the client settles the table at once (`Retired`, `Cancelled`, `Close`), so `state` is current. A retire during a call raises: it can only be a bug in the caller, and turning it into a Cancel would hide it.
- **`Close` is defined in every state** (agent): `close()` no longer waits for the call lock (§5.5, §9.3 step 4), so the call it ends feeds `Close` from a passing state; the call fails `closed`. The table's old rule "public calls arrive only at rest" now holds for `Request` and `Reset` only (test `test_requests_and_resets_arrive_only_in_resting_states`).
- **`Idle` keeps the stops** (agent): our ends lead to `Idle(stops)`, not `Crashed`, since no crash happened; the next start still waits out the backoff of earlier stops (§5.3: "after 0.5 s, 2 s, then 8 s by recent stops"). `Restarting.last` may be None for that path.
- **All our signals are SIGKILL to the whole group** (agent; §5.2 "Our end signals the whole group"): Cancel, retire, Quit anyway and close alike. A gentler end of input would leave a plugin's child that ignores SIGTERM alive, and the drain must reach "no live member" anyway; after a final reply nothing is lost.
- **A reaper thread per engine** (agent; §5.2 "drain before the reap"): the drain runs off the call path, so neither a call (the queue's `EngineLost` comes at once, S0053) nor `close()` waits for it; the end is reported once drained (`on_engine_ended`, the hook S0058 turns into `EngineEnded`/`EngineDied` by its spawn serial). The client never calls `Popen.poll` or `wait` on an engine, which would reap it behind the drain; it sets `returncode` after its own `wait4`. A new spawn waits for the previous drain (`AtMostOneEngine`), interruptibly. A drained engine that we ended wakes the call waiting for its output, so a `setsid` child that escaped the group and holds the output (§15) cannot hold a Cancel (found on self-review; test `test_a_cancel_ends_the_call_even_when_an_escaped_child_keeps_the_output_open`).
- **macOS reports a stopped child to `waitid(WEXITED | WNOWAIT)`** (found by `test_cancel_ends_a_large_request_to_a_frozen_engine`; checked by hand: `si_code` 5, `CLD_STOPPED`): a frozen engine was drained and reaped as if it had ended. `PosixGroupOps.wait_leader` now returns only on an exit report (exited, killed, dumped) and consumes a stop or continue report without reaping (`WSTOPPED | WCONTINUED | WNOHANG`), so the next wait blocks until the real exit. Test `test_a_frozen_leader_is_not_taken_for_an_exited_one`. Not in the spec's text; recorded in failure-modes' Traps.
- **"No live member" on an unexpected probe answer** (agent): any `killpg(pgid, 0)` error other than `ESRCH`/`EPERM` counts as live, so the drain never reaps on a guess (the slot stays held, which the advice of §5.2 covers).
- **The protocol, version 2** (agent; spec §5.5 names the messages, not their shape): a job message is `{v, id, op, event}` (`progress` adds a `phase`), the sidecar's answer `{v, id, op, control: "proceed"}`; the phases are the six of §5.4 as codes (`reading`, `checking_change`, `waiting_for_files`, `saving`, `updating_views`, `checking_last_change`; the UI words them). `decision {table, version, inputs, output}` is optional on both kinds of reply (spec §12: "Each engine reply that carries one adds `decision`"); its inputs are flat scalars, so no nested content can ride on it (the content-free rule itself is the engine's duty). `null` is accepted as "no decision" because the generated schema allows it; the engine never sends it. One decision per reply: a job with several engine decisions (the save of BL-0151) needs either one per reply or a list; decided there, with a version bump if needed.
- **At the gate the engine fails closed** (agent; §4, INV-01): end of input never proceeds (the read loop ends and the engine ends its group); any other line refuses the request `protocol.malformed` with nothing done past the gate, and that line is consumed. In the client, `proceed()` sends only while a gate is pending and no end of ours is recorded (the Cancel that won the commit-point step leaves nothing to proceed), since a stray `proceed` would be read as a request and put the stream out of step.
- **The violation rule after the commit point is built now** (agent; §5.3 table): it arrives with `gate`/`proceed`, otherwise this client would kill an engine past its commit point on a bad line. `ViolatedPastCommit` → `Finishing` (input closed, output discarded to the end) → `Ended` counts the stop. An exception of our own in `Finishing` (Ctrl-C) still ends the engine, as the table did in `Busy`; the journal covers that cut (BL-0151).
- **The bytecode directory is filled in the background** (agent; §5.1 says "filled once per build", not when): the first spawns of a build start without it (about 0.1-0.2 s more each) while one fill runs (`compileall` with `workers=0` under `-X pycache_prefix`, about 1 s wall on the owner's Mac, measured in this slice), in its own process group, ended by `close()`, drained like an engine. It compiles every folder on the engine's path at fill time (interpreter library, engine site-packages, the engine's source through the venv's `.pth`), never a user's folder. The directory is marked complete last and deleted mark-first. A failed fill is logged (`engine.bytecode`, `fill_failed`) and not retried until the directory is discarded. The build key hashes the interpreter, the engine site and the app version (`bytecode_build_dir`); a stale `.pyc` is safe (timestamp-checked against its source), a damaged one is the discard rule.
- **No exit line from the reaper in the log** (agent): logging from the reaper thread would interleave nondeterministically with the table's lines; the end report goes to `on_engine_ended`, and S0058's shell logs it with its job (noted on BL-0150, with the job's CPU of §5.2 for BL-0082).
- **The packaged self-check's probe keeps a hang detector** (agent): `app.selfcheck`'s view probe is a build-time check, not the engine; its `subprocess.run` timeout (30 s) replaces the retired `longest_start`. The smoke test's runner bounds the whole probe (120 s, `smoke.rs`).
- **Retired terms in two generator commands** (agent): `engine/CLAUDE.md` runs the schema and DDL generators as `python -I -m ledger_engine.…`, which the newly active term `-I -m ledger_engine` matches; they are not the engine process, so two `ALLOW` entries name them with that reason, rather than narrowing the term's pattern (a loosening).
- **Deviations from ENGINEERING.md:** EP-28 (waits on a condition with a deadline): the "this did not happen" assertions (a call still waiting, no second engine, a stopped leader not taken for an exit) look once after `ABSENCE` = 0.5 s, far above the time the event would take (as in S0053).
- **Backlog IDs used:** BL-0150 (in-progress; its row now says what S0057 built and what S0058 owns, including the end report's logging and the CPU measurement); BL-0154 (the quiesce before `close()`, cited by the TODO in `app.main`); BL-0087 (the Windows Job Object, cited by the TODO in `_AloneProcess`). No new rows.
- **Every process test has a hang guard of its own** (orchestrator, 2026-10-29: "since the client now has no time limits, a missed violation hangs forever, so every fake-engine test needs a hang guard of its own (e.g. pytest-timeout or a per-test watchdog that fails with the test name), relative and generous, not a product time limit"). What hung: the orchestrator found the mutant run's copy waiting in `test_a_job_message_out_of_order_before_the_commit_point_is_a_violation[committed-first]`; that run was the self-mutation plan, whose mutant C3 removes the "committed before proceed" check, so the hang was the mutant being caught the slow way, not a defect of the client (the check holds in the unmutated code; the test passes in 0.3 s). Built (no new dependency; pytest-timeout is not in the lock): an autouse `hang_guard` in `test_engine_client.py` and `test_engine_group.py` (`faulthandler.dump_traceback_later(15 × DEADLINE = 300 s, exit=True)`: the test process ends with every thread's traceback, which names the hung test), and `bounded_call` for the job-message tests, which ends the engine's group and fails at `DEADLINE` with what hung. The tests that wait for an end already join with `DEADLINE` and fail.
