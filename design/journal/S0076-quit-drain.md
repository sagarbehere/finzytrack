# S0076: quit waits for the drain (BL-0194, quit path)

## Brief
- Base: 959ff6d9 (records the owner's answer).
- Goal: at a normal quit and at Quit anyway, the sidecar's `EngineClient.close()` signals the engine's group and then **waits for the drain of every engine that is ending** (the one it signalled, one that had already exited or been ended, a previous engine still draining), so that the drain's later signals reach a plugin child forked during the first one (macOS) before the sidecar exits. A stuck process still cannot hold the quit: the wait is bounded (the rule is a Decision below). An engine close did not signal (a job past its commit point, §4) is not waited for: the quiesce (BL-0154) owns that wait.
- Owner decision: "Yes, make quit wait for the drain." (owner, 2026-11-07, in session; recorded on BL-0194 at 959ff6d9). It amends how spec §9.3 step 4 applies ADR-0063 decision 1 step 4 ("Every wait has an exit"); the spec's words were "without waiting for the reap".
- Implements: ADR-0063 decision 1 step 4 (amendment recorded in this slice); ADR-0064 decision 1 (one engine at a time) and its engine-lock amendment; ADR-0068 note of 2026-11-01 (the drain); spec `design/specs/engine-jobs.md` §5.1, §5.2, §5.5, §9.3 step 4, §14, §15; `design/specs/failure-modes.md` E7, S7.
- Capabilities: CAP-DATA-ENGINE-JOBS (planned; no row change).
- Invariants touched: none changed. INV-01 and INV-22 rely on one engine writing at a time; the drain and the engine lock keep that across a restart.
- **The bytes it protects:** none directly. The drain keeps the engine lock truthful across a quit: a plugin child that survives the quit holding the inherited lock blocks all ledger work at the next start until it is ended by hand or the computer restarts (fails closed); one without the lock can run beside the next engine (a plugin's own program). Not advisory in EP-30's sense (it is not a check the user is told about), and no policy can forbid plugins that fork, so no owner question.
- Risk class / lane: **Medium / full lane** (agent). It changes when the sidecar exits, not what any engine writes, nor when the next engine may start (the engine lock still decides that). Its failure mode, a wait that ends before the drain, is today's behaviour. No data-safety red team: the mechanism neither writes ledger or user data nor gates a write (PROCESS.md §4, ADR-0068 D6). The verifier may raise it.
- Acceptance tests (`backend/tests/platform/test_engine_client.py`):
  - must-not-pass on 959ff6d9: `test_close_waits_for_the_drain_of_the_engine_it_ended` (a fake reap held: `close()` has not returned while the drain is under way, returns once it is done); `test_after_a_close_the_plugins_child_is_gone_and_the_lock_free` (real engine, `test.child` holding a lock: at `close()`'s return the child is gone and the lock free; repeated); `test_close_waits_for_a_previous_engine_still_draining`; `test_close_waits_for_the_drain_of_an_engine_that_died_by_itself`; `test_the_quit_waits_for_the_drain_at_most_the_display_delay` (the bound is the scheduler's `DISPLAY_DELAY`; fails on the base at import);
  - the bound: `test_close_stops_waiting_for_a_drain_that_does_not_end` (returns while the drain never ends, after waiting at least the bound; replaces `test_close_returns_without_waiting_for_the_reap`); `test_close_returns_while_a_call_waits_on_an_engine_our_signal_cannot_end` kept;
  - the exclusion: `test_a_cancel_or_close_past_the_commit_point_lets_the_job_finish[close]` gains "close() returned while the job still ran" (passes on the base: a `@regression-guard` line).
- Expected doc impact: ADR-0063 (amendment with the owner's quote); spec §5.1, §5.2, §5.5, §9.3 step 4, §14, §15; failure-modes E7 (text and test names), S7 (the sidecar's exit now waits for the drain, bounded); ARCHITECTURE.md (the `close()` lines of the supervision paragraph, the product paragraph and the bounded-state table); ADR-0068 (a pointer note); BL-0194 (quit part done; the watchdog case stays open); docstrings and comments (`engine_client`, `engine.EnginePort.close`, `main`, `state_registry`). No user docs, no API change, no generated artifact.
- Open questions: none for the owner. The bound is the agent's (delegated by the orchestrator); see Decisions.

## Decisions
- **The bound is the display delay (2 s), not "no bound" and not a new number** (agent; the orchestrator delegated it). A stuck process and one still exiting look the same from outside (ADR-0064's premise), so any rule that lets the quit go on without the user is a bound of some kind. The drain normally ends in milliseconds: measured here with a real engine and a `test.child` holding a lock, 0.8 to 1.7 ms after `close()` (20 runs, `scratchpad/s0076/measure.py`); a 1 GiB member frees its lock 20 to 27 ms after SIGKILL (S0075). A drain still going after 2 s is waiting for a process stuck in the kernel, which no wait or signal ends. The display delay is already the product's moment for "a signalled process that has not ended may be stuck" (§5.2's advice), so reusing it adds no new number; a test keeps `CLOSE_WAIT` equal to the scheduler's `DISPLAY_DELAY` (the adapter does not import the scheduler; the composition root does not need to pass it). A wait cut at the bound leaves exactly the earlier behaviour (one signal, drain cut with the sidecar), never a loss. "No fixed time budgets" is about gates; this is product behaviour with its rule stated (spec §9.3 step 4).
- **Rejected: no bound, the user's exit** (the shell's planned "still closing… Quit now", a further SIGTERM in headless mode). It needs no number, but a stuck process would hold every quit until the user acts, which the owner's condition ("so long as a stuck process still cannot hold the quit") excludes. **Rejected: a progress rule** (stop once drain rounds find no new member): a member in the middle of a fork when signalled can still add a child after such a round, so it needs a round count (a time in disguise) and hooks into the drain loop. Both are in the ADR-0063 amendment.
- **Which engines a close waits for** (agent): the previous engine if its drain is not done; the running one if we ended it (`reason` set: `close`, or an earlier `quit_anyway`, `cancel`) or it has exited by itself (the drain also ends its members). Not a running engine the close left alone past a job's commit point (§4): waiting for that job is the quiesce's (BL-0154, with its texts and Quit anyway), and the bound would cut it anyway. The exit check is `_mark_ended`'s own (`exited` or a non-blocking `leader_exited`).
- **The wait lives in `close()`, not in `app.main`** (agent): every closer (the quit, the packaged self-check, a user container's close) gets one rule; the self-check pays the drain's milliseconds. `close()` still takes no call lock and still never waits for a call; the wait is on the drained events under `_signals`, which the reaper sets and notifies. Signal handlers (`StopRequest`, main thread) only record, so they run during the timed wait and cannot deadlock it.
- **The ADR's words** (agent): "without waiting for the reap" is the spec's application (§9.3 step 4), not ADR-0063's text; the ADR's step 4 says "Every wait has an exit (agent)". The amendment is recorded in ADR-0063 (status line and a dated section with the owner's quote, the bound, the rejected alternatives) and a pointer note in ADR-0068, whose note of 2026-11-01 said "so does a quit's end".
- **Nothing new is logged** (agent): a drain cut at the bound is not an event. What the user would see of it, the next start waiting at the engine lock, already has its advice; a new event would change the event schema for no reader.
- **The shell's "still closing" (S7, planned, BL-0095)** now overlaps: the sidecar may wait up to the display delay for a stuck drain, so the shell's text can show for a moment before the sidecar exits by itself. Stated in spec §15 ("Closing the app"); left to BL-0095's design.
- **The real-process test probes the lock first** (agent): its first version asked `gone(child)` first, which runs `ps` (milliseconds), and passed on the base because the drain finished meanwhile. With the `flock` probe first it fails on the base at run 0 in 3 of 3 runs, both parameters ("run 0: the close returned before the drain").
- **Base failures** (959ff6d9): `test_close_waits_for_the_drain_of_the_engine_it_ended`, `..._a_previous_engine_still_draining`, `..._of_an_engine_that_died_by_itself`, `test_close_stops_waiting_for_a_drain_that_does_not_end` fail at the new `close_wait` keyword (TypeError) and `test_the_quit_waits_for_the_drain_at_most_the_display_delay` at the import of `CLOSE_WAIT`; their behaviour is pinned by the mutants below. `test_after_a_close_the_plugins_child_is_gone_and_the_lock_free[close|quit anyway, then close]` fail on behaviour. The added assertion in `test_a_cancel_or_close_past_the_commit_point_lets_the_job_finish[close]` ("close() returned while the job still ran") passes on the base: a `@regression-guard` line for the exclusion, killed by its mutant.
