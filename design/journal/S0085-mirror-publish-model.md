# S0085: The mirror's generation publish, as rules and a durability model

## Brief
- Goal: the first half of BL-0113. Write the design of the mirror's generation publish as rules (`design/specs/mirror/publish.md`, linked from the mirror spec): the folder and what is ours, generation names, the build into its own name, the refusal of an incomplete build, the syncs, the link swap, the stale-journal rule, what `meta` carries (BL-0125), the cleanup with the grace period and the link rule, what a reader does, what the save's gate compares, and a crash at every step with what the next start sees. Then its TLA+ model, durability only (EP-19): `ReadersSeeCompleteGeneration` and `SnapshotIsSomeCommittedState`, a seeded mutant per property, vacuity witnesses, a two-way mapping to the rules and to the planned code functions (S0086), and the small tier run green. No product code: S0086 builds it.
- Implements: ADR-0015 (with its amendments of 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-29, and the amendment of 2026-11-07 this slice records), ADR-0016 (the reads), ADR-0064 (one engine, one job at a time), ADR-0068 D1 (the rebuild after a save is the publish's seam); spec `design/specs/engine-jobs.md` §6.1 step 4 (the gate), §6.7, §7; EP-19, EP-30; PROCESS.md §3 "Designs checked by a formal model".
- Capabilities: CAP-DATA-MIRROR (stays partial: the design lands, the code is S0086), CAP-MIRROR-EXTERNAL (the stable path an outside tool opens: `current.db`).
- Invariants touched: INV-05 (the mirror is a rebuildable cache, published atomically); INV-10 (bounded caches: the generations kept); INV-23 (nothing private in the model or docs).
- Risk class / lane: High (the save's gate compares the published mirror, so the publish gates a write) / full lane. No data-safety red team: the slice adds no write path (design and model only, PROCESS.md §4); the red team comes with S0086's code.
- The bytes it protects: no ledger bytes (the publish writes only the app's cache folder). It keeps two outcomes truthful: what the screens and outside tools read is one whole generation built from one state the ledger really had, and the save's gate compares the plan with the generation that is really published.
- Acceptance tests (new; they fail on the base, which has neither the spec section nor the model): `scripts/tests/test_tla_mirror_publish.py` (the property, mutant and witness names in step between the spec, the model and the configuration table, both ways; every property has a mutant; MAPPING.md lists exactly the model's actions and maps each one to a rule of the spec and a code function; the configurations are generated). The model's small tier (`formal/tla/mirror-publish/run_all.sh --tier S`): every base world passes, every mutant breaks its property, every witness is reached.
- Expected doc impact: new `design/specs/mirror/publish.md`; `design/specs/mirror/README.md` (Orientation, link); ADR-0015 amendment (2026-11-07); `design/specs/engine-jobs.md` §6.1 step 4, §6.7, §7 (pointers to the publish rules); `design/ARCHITECTURE.md` §8.3 (the second model), the `mirror/` package line; `design/ENGINEERING.md` EP-19 (where the model lives); `formal/tla/mirror-publish/` (model, MAPPING.md, HOW_TO_RUN.md, run_all.sh, gen_cfgs.py); the TLC runner shared by both models (`formal/tla/run_model.sh`; `formal/tla/engine-jobs/run_all.sh` becomes a wrapper, behaviour unchanged); `design/backlog.md` (BL-0113 split: the design done here, the row kept for S0086's code with the list of what it builds; the Windows pointer file a new M11 row, BL-0206; BL-0125, BL-0110 pointers); `design/specs/roadmap.md` §1. No user docs (nothing user-visible changes).
- Open questions: none blocking this slice. One for the owner, blocking only S0086's cleanup rule: the disk the kept generations take during a burst of saves (Decisions, Q1; `design/inbox/20261108-mirror-generations-disk.md`).

## Decisions
- **Owner decision 1, ownership of the mirror folder** (owner, 2026-11-07, in session, relayed by the orchestrator): **"Whole folder is ours"**: the publish and its cleanup may delete anything in `…/workspaces/<key>/mirror/` and replace whatever sits at `current.db`. The orchestrator's safety rule, stated to the owner with it: the cleanup never follows a link; a link at `mirror/` itself or inside it is removed as a link, never what it points to.
- **Owner decision 2, reader pinning** (owner, 2026-11-07, in session, relayed by the orchestrator): **"Grace period only (Recommended)"**: ADR-0015's amendment is the whole rule: the previous generation is always kept, a replaced one is kept 10 minutes, then deleted; no pin tracking in the app (on macOS and Linux, removing a file that an open connection reads is safe); a read resolves `current.db` once and opens that file. Windows' pointer file and retry-on-locked-delete become a backlog row for M11 (ADR-0061).
- Both are recorded as ADR-0015's amendment of 2026-11-07 (rubric item 4 is satisfied by the owner's own answers).
- **Own model, not an extension of engine-jobs** (agent): the two designs share only "one engine job at a time" and the `.open` journal; the publish's world (generations, a link, readers, a power loss per folder entry) would multiply the engine-jobs model's states for no property of either. BL-0110's row already said the publish gets its own. Both run over one shared TLC runner (`formal/tla/run_model.sh`, factored out of `engine-jobs/run_all.sh`, whose arguments and results are unchanged: its meta-test passes).
- **Names and numbers** (agent): `gen-<N>.db`; the next number above every number still in any name (`gen-<N>.db-*` included), the current one and the sidecar's floor (the highest number it served this session). Why the floor: ADR-0037 orders `ledger_version`, so a cache purge under a running app must not send the number back. Why journals count: a number is never reused while a journal of it remains (§5); the model's `reuse_number` mutant shows the property breaks without it.
- **SQLite resolves links** (agent, evidence): SQLite 3.53 on macOS names the journals of a database opened through `current.db` after the file the link resolves to (`gen-1.db-wal`, `gen-1.db-journal`; script in the session scratchpad, `sltest.py`). So the stale-WAL hazard of ADR-0015 needs no rule beyond fresh names and readers that open the generation; the cleanup still removes `current.db-*` for programs with an SQLite that does not resolve links.
- **The cleanup runs first in every `open` and `rebuild` job, nowhere else** (agent; EP-30): one place, before the load, so never beside a build of its own; a cleanup after the publish too would only remove what aged in the seconds of one job. Keep set: `current.db` when it names a regular `gen-<C>.db`, the current and previous generations, and generations whose replacement is younger than the grace; the replacement time is the next-higher generation file's modification time, which is the replacement time because files above the current number are removed before any build. A journal beside a kept generation is left (removing it could make an outside writer's half-done change permanent; readers refuse the file and the open rebuilds). No second link for "previous": the highest number below the current one is it.
- **The save's gate reads the published generation itself** (agent; engine-jobs §6.1 step 4 already planned it): the engine resolves `current.db` once inside the save job and compares that generation's fingerprint digest with the plan's; `SaveRequest.mirror` goes in S0086. One job at a time makes the read and the commit point atomic with respect to publishes.
- **`meta`** (agent, BL-0125): `ledger_version` = `<N>/<digest>`; `fingerprint` = `ledger_set.encode`; new keys `engine_build` (the S0050 note on BL-0113: a row-logic change with the same DDL must rebuild) and `as_of` (the build's today: `account_balances` and `lots` stop at it). Adding `meta` keys needs no ADR (mirror README rule 2); S0086 adds them with `surface.toml`'s note.
- **BL-0113 stays the code row** (agent): the code's `TODO(BL-0113)` comments keep pointing at the open work; the row says the design is done here and lists what S0086 builds. Windows is BL-0206 (M11, ADR-0061, the owner's words of 2026-11-07).
- **S0086 needs BL-0120 first** (agent, found here): `rows.COMPLETE` is False until the `commodities` rows exist, so every build says `build_complete = 0` and §4 step 4 refuses to publish it. BL-0120's owner question is answered (2026-10-29); its code is not built. Recorded in BL-0113's row and the roadmap.
- **Q1, escalated** (rubric item 4: it would change ADR-0015's amendment): the grace period keeps every generation replaced in the last 10 minutes, about 80 MB each at 54k entries (S0050), and a rebuild follows every save, so a burst of saves can keep gigabytes. Not decided here; `design/inbox/20261108-mirror-generations-disk.md` (recommendation: at most 2 older generations besides the current and previous). It blocks only S0086's cleanup cap; the model does not depend on it (its cleanup may remove any file it does not keep, at any time).
- **Model abstractions** (agent; MAPPING.md §3): the load is the rebuild's (stamped with the observation before it, re-observed before the swap); the overlay load of the open is the special case. A stale journal at a reused number is assumed to corrupt the new file (conservative). The folder's own shape, time, the floor, availability and Windows are not modelled; S0086's tests check them.
- **Engineering principles:** no deviation.
- **Tests first:** `scripts/tests/test_tla_mirror_publish.py` was written before the model and the spec section and failed on the base (2 tests run, 4 failures and 1 error: no `formal/tla/mirror-publish/`, no `publish.md`); it passes now (9 tests). The model's small tier was first run on the finished model: a mutant (`reresolve_per_read`) first passed on `S_publish`, whose ledger never changes, so every generation was alike; it moved to `S_ledger`, where it breaks its property.

## Hand-off (PROCESS.md §3 step 4; ADR-0057)
(The verifier reads only the Brief, so this section does not steer its attacks; it is the implementer's own record.)
- Checks that accept, tag or refuse, each with its must-not-pass test:
  - the model's `ReadersSeeCompleteGeneration` → mutants `no_file_sync`, `publish_incomplete`, `build_in_place`, `reuse_number`, each reported violated by TLC (`results/SUMMARY.md`; each counterexample read: a power loss after an unsynced swap, an incomplete file published, a reader of a half-rewritten live file, a cut build's journal played into a reused number);
  - the model's `SnapshotIsSomeCommittedState` → mutants `no_recheck` (rows from the edited file, `meta` naming the earlier one), `no_open_check` (a half-done save published), `reresolve_per_read` (a request's two reads from two generations);
  - each base world's pass is not vacuous: 9 witnesses reached (MAPPING.md §4.2), among them a read of a generation whose file was removed, a read after a power loss and a link put back on an older generation by a power loss;
  - `scripts/tests/test_tla_mirror_publish.py` (tooling) → its must-not-pass cases by self-mutation below.
- Self-mutation (`scripts/mutants.py`, plan `s0085-mutants.toml` in the session scratchpad; command: the meta-test module): 7 mutants, 7 killed: a MAPPING.md action row dropped, a row naming no code, a third property named in §10, a mutant missing from §10, a property left without mutants, a row citing a section the spec does not have, a witness renamed in the model. The model's own mutants are the seeded bugs above (7 of 7 violate their property).
- Small tier on this model (sha256 4ef12f36ab63, 8 workers, the owner's Mac): 19 configurations, all as expected, about 19 s: `S_publish` 53,342 distinct states (depth 73), `S_power` 80,227 (57), `S_ledger` 28,450 (41); mutants 240 to 24,736 states; witnesses 128 to 19,237. `M_all`, `L_all`: not run (the owner's, BL-0110).
- Population: every state a crash can leave in the mirror folder, built by behaviour from the effects the publish makes, in their order (§2, §4, §6; the build's effects read from `ledger_engine.mirror.build.build` as built: exclusive create, one transaction, removal of its file and journal on an error): E0 the folder (a link or file at `mirror/` removed, the folder made, its parent synced), E1 one cleanup removal (a database file, a journal, a link, a folder, another entry), E2 the exclusive create, E3 the transaction's writes (journal and pages), E4 the commit (journal deleted), E4' the build's own removal after an error, E5 the file's sync, E6 the folder's sync, E7 the temporary link, E8 the rename over `current.db`, E9 the folder's sync. In: 12 cut points (before E0, after each of the 11 effects) × 2 (a death of the engine or the app; a power loss) = 24 cases. Out: 11 folder states: (1) clean (current, previous, kept ones); (2) earlier entries partly removed or back after a power loss; (3) the folder replaced and still missing; (4) a journal without its file; (5) `gen-<N>` and its journal mid-build; (6) `gen-<N>` torn or missing after a power loss; (7) `gen-<N>` whole, not published (committed, refused incomplete, moved or `.open`), synced or not; (8) `gen-<N>` whole with its journal back after a power loss; (9) the temporary link; (10) `current.db` on `gen-<N>`, published; (11) `current.db` back on the older generation with a whole `gen-<N>` above it. Each is a row (or the first row's text) of spec §9 with what readers see and what the next job does; in none is a reader led to a partial, incomplete or torn file. States 1, 2, 4 to 8, 10 and 11 are reached by the model (witnesses `W_TornLeft`, `W_LinkReverted`, `W_IncompleteRefused` among them); 3 and 9 are not modelled (MAPPING.md §3: readers never resolve to either) and are S0086's tests.
