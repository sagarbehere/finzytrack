# S0037: beanquery in the ledger engine (BL-0081)

## Brief
- Goal: beanquery 0.2.0 (ARCHITECTURE.md §10's pin) joins the ledger engine's environment with its dependency TatSu-LTS as resolved (5.16.0 under the 14-day age rule), by the owner's decision of 2026-10-26, so BQL work is not held by the open licence question. The licence check lists TatSu-LTS under `[engine.reviewed]` pending legal advice (like `regex` and `python-dateutil`), and `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES` carries TatSu's required acknowledgment verbatim. Because TatSu's metadata says only "BSD License", the check also learns to read the licence texts of the Python packages it judges and to refuse a bundled package whose licence asks for an acknowledgment that the notices do not carry (that is how TatSu's clause was nearly missed in S0035). The licence question itself becomes a backlog row for the ADR-0042 legal review before v0.4.0; ADR-0054 gets an amendment note.
- Implements: ADR-0054 decisions 1 and 5 (beanquery in the engine; the engine's reviewed list, amended), ADR-0042 (notices, licence check, legal advice before v0.4.0), ADR-0004 (Python 3.13, Beancount 3.2.0), ARCHITECTURE.md §2.0 and §10.

## Decisions
- **Owner decision, verbatim** (2026-10-26, in session): "Please include the bin query in the engine, sorting out the license issues, make that a backlog item. I don't want functionality to be compromised during development because of legal questions." ("bin query" = beanquery.) It answers `design/inbox/answered/20261026-beanquery-tatsu-licence.md` with option 1 and amends ADR-0054 decision 5 (amendment section appended, 2026-10-26). The licence question is BL-0085 (M12, the ADR-0042 legal review), together with `regex`'s CNRI-Python part noted by the S0035 verifier. BL-0081 is closed.
- **Versions** (agent): beanquery 0.2.0 is ARCHITECTURE.md §10's pin and still PyPI's latest (2025-03-24); `scripts/oracle` pins no beanquery, so §10 is the reference. The lock resolved TatSu-LTS 5.16.0 (2026-01-14, TatSu-LTS has no required dependencies); click 8.5.0 and python-dateutil 2.9.0.post0 were already locked for Beancount. Both new packages are far older than the 14-day rule (`exclude-newer = "14 days"`). The legacy app ran beanquery 0.2.0 with TatSu-LTS 5.13.2; the smoke test shows 0.2.0 parses and runs with 5.16.0.
- **SPDX ids** (agent): TatSu-LTS's wheel licence is the 3-clause BSD text plus clause 4, "Redistributions of any form whatsoever must retain the following acknowledgment: ...", which is SPDX `BSD-3-Clause-Attribution` (the SPDX template's clause 4 is word for word the same, with another name). It is not on the engine's allowed list and passes only as a reviewed package. beanquery's wheel carries the GPL version 2 text with no "or later" and its modules say `__license__ = "GNU GPLv2"`: `GPL-2.0-only`. Both reported licences are trove-classifier names, so both get `[mapping]` entries for their exact versions (BL-0050).
- **The acknowledgment rule** (agent; a tightening of the licence check, which agents may apply, ADR-0042 "reviewed like any rule change"): TatSu's clause was nearly missed in S0035 because pip-licenses reports only "BSD License". The check now asks pip-licenses for each Python package's licence and notice texts (`--with-license-file --with-notice-file --no-license-path`) and finds every "following acknowledg(e)ment" clause. A bundled package with such a clause needs `[acknowledgment.<package>]` (version, text, source) whose text equals the sentence the licence quotes (whitespace normalized), so a shortened or paraphrased text is refused; the notices carry it as `  Acknowledgment: ...` under the package's line. An entry for another version, for a package not bundled, or for one whose text was not read is refused. Only the Python groups are read: JavaScript and Rust texts are BL-0086 (M11). Today the clause occurs only in TatSu-LTS: the backend venv (32 packages) and the engine venv (27 before this slice) have no other licence or notice text with "acknowledg" (scan of pip-licenses output, 2026-10-26).
- **Notices scope** (agent): `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES` still lists names and licences only; shipping the full licence texts in the bundles (BSD clause 2 for every BSD package, not only TatSu) is CAP-DESK-LICENSE-NOTICES at M11. The acknowledgment line is what the owner asked for now.
- **The reviewed-package warning** now reads "reviewed engine dependency, ADR-0054; pending legal advice" (it said "dependency of Beancount", which TatSu-LTS is not: beanquery needs it).
- **No change to `scripts/check_engine_boundary.py`** (agent): rules 1 and 2 already treat `beanquery` like `beancount` (`BEANCOUNT = {"beancount", "beanquery"}`), with must-not-pass cases in `scripts/tests/test_engine_boundary.py` (`from beanquery import query`, `import_module('beanquery')`) and the main side's `find_spec("beanquery") is None` and lockfile tests in `backend/tests/architecture/test_contracts.py`. TatSu is not added to the boundary: it is an implementation detail of beanquery, licence-judged by the check above, and absent from the main sidecar's lock.
- **No gateway code** (agent): `ledger_engine/ledger/beancount_port.py` does not exist yet and no BQL operation is in scope. The smoke test lives in `engine/tests/` (the engine's tests may import Beancount and beanquery). It runs the BQL probe in a child `python -I` and reads JSON back, because beanquery ships no type information and parts of Beancount's API are partially unknown to pyright: a file-level `# pyright: basic` was tried first and dropped, since EP-22 keeps pyright strict and loosening it needs the owner. Local stubs under `engine/typings/` and the typed facade come with the first BQL slice (CAP-ENGINE-BQL).
- **Tests first** (base ea9133da, run in a scratch export of the base with the new test files and the base lock synced): `test_check_licences.py` fails at import on the base (`check_licences` has no `Acknowledgment`), so every test in it fails; `engine/tests/test_beanquery_env.py` (2 tests) and `test_the_engine_environment_has_beanquery` fail (no beanquery). ## Verifier

### Fix round 1
- F1 (class-closing): the check no longer takes texts from pip-licenses. `read_texts` runs inside each venv (`.venv/bin/python -I scripts/check_licences.py --read-texts`) and reads, per distribution, every `License-File` it declares (under `licenses/`, or beside METADATA before PEP 639, as python-dateutil 2.9.0.post0 does) and every RECORD entry in the dist-info's `licenses/` folder or named LICENSE/LICENCE/COPYING/NOTICE anywhere (mdurl, sortedcontainers and pyright ship licences only that way). It parses RECORD itself: `Distribution.files` drops listed files that are missing (Python 3.12+), found when the missing-file test first passed for the wrong reason. A declared or listed file that cannot be read stops the check; a bundled package the reader did not see at its exact version is refused ("was not read"), with or without an entry. A bundled package whose texts are empty is refused unless `[no_licence_text.<package>]` names its exact version with a reason; beancount 3.2.0 is listed (its wheel has no licence file; METADATA `License: GPL-2.0-only`, `__license__ = "GNU GPLv2"`, upstream LICENSE GPL version 2, no acknowledgment clause). An entry for another version, for a package that now ships text, or for one not bundled is refused. Docstring and ARCHITECTURE.md's licence paragraph state the reach and the limits. Tests: `ReadTexts` (6, fixture distributions written to a temp folder, one run through the script in another interpreter), `NoLicenceText` (6), `EngineWiring.test_python_deps_reads_every_licence_text_of_bundled_packages_in_the_venv`, `test_python_deps_keeps_no_text_for_another_version`, `test_python_deps_fails_closed_on_an_unreadable_licence_file`, `test_main_refuses_a_bundled_package_without_licence_text`, `Acknowledgments.test_a_bundled_package_whose_text_was_not_read_is_refused_without_an_entry_too`, `Config.test_beancount_is_reviewed_as_shipping_no_licence_text_for_its_exact_version`, `test_a_malformed_no_licence_text_entry_is_refused`. The verifier's F1 fixture now gives two refusals (twolic's vendored clause, nolic's missing text).
- F2: texts are kept per process (`{group: {package: text}}`), each bundled package is judged in its own group with that group's version and text, and notes are keyed `(group, package)` so the notices carry an acknowledgment only under the process whose licence asked for it. Tests: `PerProcess` (2), `EngineWiring.test_main_judges_each_process_with_its_own_texts` (the repro's two-version case), `Acknowledgments.test_a_note_belongs_to_its_own_group`; the verifier's F2 repro now exits 1 with lib 1.0's refusal.

The unquoted sentence now ends at the first full stop that ends a line (or the text) unless the next line goes on in lower case, so "Foo Inc. and contributors." and its wrapped form are read whole, and the 4-clause BSD form (sentence, then "Alternately, ..." on the next line, CRLF included) still ends at its own full stop: `test_an_unquoted_sentence_with_an_internal_full_stop_is_read_whole` (also "Foo Inc." alone refused), `test_an_unquoted_sentence_ends_at_the_full_stop_that_ends_its_line`. ## Retro
- What the verifier found: the acknowledgment rule trusted a tool's notion of "the licence file" (pip-licenses reads one) and read an empty text as "no clause". Both are the same class: an absent input judged as a passing input. The fix reads the population itself and makes every absence a refusal or a reviewed, versioned exception.
- What a cheaper gate could have found: a population count in the hand-off ("distributions with more than one licence file: n; with none: n") would have shown packaging's three files and beancount's none during the slice. The hand-off's population line counted packages with the clause, not the inputs the scan depended on; the inputs should be counted too.
- Where time went: building the reader and finding that `Distribution.files` hides missing RECORD entries (caught by the missing-file test, not by reading the docs).
- Process cost: one implementer pass for the slice, one verifier round, one fix pass.
