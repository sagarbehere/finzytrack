# BL-0256: option (b) cannot turn a refusal into a success; keep it, or close the row as a stated limit?
- Asked: 2026-11-14 by the orchestrator, slice S0118 (the implementer's stop before coding)
- blocks: S0118 (BL-0256's half; BL-0255 can go ahead without it)
- Rubric item: 4 (changing an ADR: option (b) needs the ADR-0064 amendment to decision 2's ordering rule), and the owner's 2026-11-14 decision on BL-0256 rested on a premise that does not hold
- ADRs checked: ADR-0064 decision 2 (the ordering rule: waited-for first), its amendments and notes; ADR-0068 D1; none settles whether to keep a decision made on a false premise

## Context (plain language, a concrete example first)
Example: you save change A. Its background rebuild (the update of the views) is queued. A second later you preview change B, and the preview waits in the queue too.

BL-0256 (from the M1a area review, before the preview existed) said that if B waited for A's rebuild it "would have succeeded". On 2026-11-14 you chose option (b) on that basis: "For Q1, let's go with option b."

Since S0117 that premise is false. A preview and a confirm are each tied to one numbered version of the views (the mirror generation). The number is fixed **when the job is queued** (`app.workspaces.writes.submit_preview` takes `generation` from the caller; `_confirm_work` refuses `PREVIEW_STALE` when the published number differs from the plan's). A preview or confirm queued before A's rebuild ran cannot name the version that rebuild will make, because it does not exist yet. So letting the rebuild go first only changes which refusal B gets, and B waits the rebuild's length before it is refused.

The implementer showed this on the real engine with a test copy, patching the promotion in for the second run of each case:

| Case | Today's order | With (b) |
|---|---|---|
| Preview B queued behind confirm A | refused `WORKSPACE_NOT_READY` (views not fresh), 0.9 s | rebuild runs first, then refused `WORKSPACE_NOT_READY` (another version), 3.5 s |
| Confirm B queued behind confirm A | refused `LEDGER_EXTERNALLY_CHANGED` | refused `PREVIEW_STALE` ("preview again") |

So the one real gain of (b) is a truer message for the second confirm. Today it blames an outside change for our own save A; with (b) it says "preview again". In both cases the user previews again. BL-0255 (in the same slice, unaffected) makes that preview again succeed once the rebuild has run.

## Options (cheapest first; PROCESS.md §5)
1. **Close BL-0256 as a stated limit.** One line in engine-jobs §3: a preview or confirm made before a save's rebuild ran is refused, and the user previews again. No queue change, no ADR-0064 amendment. S0118 builds BL-0255 only.
2. **Keep (b) for its wording effect.** Build the promotion and the ADR-0064 amendment as decided, with the brief's claims corrected: the second confirm says "preview again" instead of "changed outside Finzytrack", and every such preview or confirm waits the rebuild's length (about 2 s at 54k) before it is refused.
3. **Fix the confirm's wording without touching the queue.** The confirm would tell a `ledger_changed` caused by this session's own earlier save apart from an outside change, and say "preview again". This is not designed yet; it is a new mechanism with its own brief and a backlog row.

## Recommendation
Option 1. (b) changes the queue's pure table (`app.platform.engine_jobs.queue`) and buys only a wording change. That mechanism has this history: S0053 needed two verifier fix rounds (round 1 F1-F9; round 2 R2-F1-R2-F3, R2-F1 a stall caused by round 1's own fix), S0058 one round with a High (F1), S0069 a High from the step-back area review (ADR-0068 D8), S0080 one round. If the misleading `LEDGER_EXTERNALLY_CHANGED` on a second confirm matters to you, option 3 can follow as its own backlog row, without touching the queue.
